## What is FabriX?

FabriX (pronounced like the English word “fabrics”, /ˈfæbrɪks/) is a mod for FS25 and was developed to deepen and extend the existing production system.
In the base game, productions follow the principle "goods in, factory on, goods out".
Production costs are deducted automatically, without you having any influence on them.

FabriX redesigns exactly this process! It reworks the entire production system and turns it into a management area of its own.

### What changes in detail?

The fixed production costs of the base game are removed. Instead, every factory now uses electricity and water, e.g. for lighting, cleaning or heating and cooling the products. You pay for what you use.
On top of that, the property tax is charged once a year and your employees' salaries every month. Transport costs in "Distribute" mode are still charged as usual, but are now explicitly shown as transport costs.

Without employees, there is no production. How much a factory produces depends on who works in its shifts.
The more experienced or qualified your employees are, the more productive your factory is.
From time to time it can also be upgraded to create more storage capacity and more workplaces.

You now have to hire employees, who can have different qualifications, and train apprentices. By working, your staff gain experience (XP), level up and thus become more productive. Note that their salary also rises automatically.

### Your tasks

Organising and managing your factories is now entirely in your hands.
Find the right employees, create shift plans, train your own apprentices and send your skilled workers on further training.
Keep your factories busy at all times, because employees are always paid, even when your production stands still.
You should also always keep an eye on your running costs for salaries, water, electricity, transport and property tax.

## The FabriX menu

Press **K** to open and close the menu. You can remap the key in the game settings under **Controls**. The menu has four tabs:

- **Overview:** Shows the key figures at a glance: employees per category, your factories, used and vacant shift slots, productivity and running costs. It also shows warnings: missing Master or Director, vacant shift slots, running dismissals, sick employees, upcoming retirements, apprentices without a shift and employees ready for training. Under "Notifications" you can switch off the messages about promotion & training, sickness and staff changes individually. The setting only applies to you.
- **Shift plan:** Select a factory and view its three shifts. Fill vacant slots with suitable employees, remove employees again and fill the leadership roles. The productivity of each shift is shown directly. At the top right you see the factory's level and experience; press **X** to start the upgrade and **C** to remove all employees from the factory.
- **Employees:** Overview of your staff with level, experience, salary, age and workplace. Here you start training, dismiss employees, withdraw dismissals or enter salary negotiations if an employee has resigned on their own. Not every employee accepts your offer. The "Sick" section shows who is currently off.
- **Job market:** Lists applicants, split into seasonal workers, apprentices and skilled workers. Each hire costs a fee of €250. The hiring fee and the severance on dismissal are meant to stop you from hiring employees only when you need them and letting them go again afterwards. Your employees have families and depend on a permanent job. So it is often cheaper to keep them employed even when you have no work for them.

## Shifts

Every factory can produce around the clock, provided the three 8-hour shifts are staffed:

| Shift       | Time        |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| Early shift | 00:00–08:00 |
| Day shift   | 08:00–16:00 |
| Late shift  | 16:00–24:00 |

- The number of slots per shift equals the factory level: level 1 has 1 slot per shift, level 5 has 5.
- An employee can only work one shift per day. Once their shift has started, they cannot be assigned elsewhere that day. This also applies if you remove them during their running shift.
- A shift's productivity is the sum of the productivity of all assigned employees. The more and the more experienced employees a shift has, the faster the factory produces.
- A productivity of 100% equals the regular number of cycles per month in the base game. Three employees with 120% each make the factory produce 3.6 times as fast during that shift. If a shift is empty, the factory stands still during that time.
- A factory's Master also works in the day shift and adds to its productivity.
- Employees who are in training cannot be assigned to a shift.

## Employees

Employees gain experience (XP) while working their shift in a running production. A full working month yields about **10 XP**. With enough XP they level up and become more productive. The salary also rises with the level. Apprentices and skilled workers gain no XP while in training or during a running notice period.

The job market offers three categories:

- **Seasonal workers** can be used right away and in any shift. They level up to 10 and become steadily more productive.
- **Apprentices** are cheap but not very productive at first. They only work the day shift and only in factories at level 3 or higher, because there is always a Master there who can train them. New apprentices are only available in **July, August and September**, at the start of the training year, and only once at least one of your factories has reached level 3.
- **Skilled workers** progress through **Journeyman**, **Master** and **Director**. The job market mostly offers journeymen; Masters and Directors are rare.

The job market is topped up every month. At the start of each season (March, June, September, December) it is cleared completely and filled anew. The number of applicants depends on how many factories you own:

| Factories | Seasonal workers | Skilled workers | Apprentices (Jul–Sep) |
| --------- | ---------------: | --------------: | --------------------: |
| 0–2       |                4 |               3 |                     5 |
| 3–4       |                6 |               5 |                     9 |
| 5–6       |                8 |               7 |                    12 |
| 7–10      |               10 |              10 |                    17 |
| 11+       |               10 |              10 |                    20 |

Seasonal workers are 30–55 years old and start at level 1–3. Apprentices are 16–21 years old, skilled workers 25–35. The base salary is €100 for seasonal workers and €80 for apprentices and skilled workers. From the job market, seasonal workers negotiate an extra 10–40%, skilled workers 10–70%. Apprentices get no surcharge and keep their base salary after promotion to Journeyman, Master or Director. Salaries are paid at the end of the month.

### Seasonal workers

Seasonal workers are your flexible core staff. Their level directly affects productivity. An experienced seasonal worker produces almost 50% more than a new one. Their salary rises in the same proportion: the salary factor equals the productivity.

| Level | XP to next level | Productivity |
| ----- | ---------------: | -----------: |
| 1     |               50 |         100% |
| 2     |               60 |         105% |
| 3     |               70 |         110% |
| 4     |               80 |         115% |
| 5     |               90 |         120% |
| 6     |              100 |         125% |
| 7     |              110 |         130% |
| 8     |              120 |         135% |
| 9     |              130 |         140% |
| 10    |              max |         145% |

### Apprentices & skilled workers

Apprentices and skilled workers follow a career path. An apprentice starts at level 1 and becomes a Journeyman at level 4. From then on you can train them further: first to Master, then to Director. This is your decision; a Journeyman can also stay a Journeyman for life. For the leadership slots, however, you need Masters and Directors.

Once an employee has collected the required XP, they go into training for the listed time. For apprentices this happens automatically. You start Master training and Director training yourself in the **Employees** tab and pay a fee. The employee only reaches the next level after training.

Between training periods, Journeymen (4 → 5 → 6) and Masters (7 → 8 → 9) level up automatically once they have enough XP. You cannot start training while a dismissal is running.

The salary is the base salary multiplied by the salary factor of the current level.

| Level | Role       | XP to next level | Productivity | Salary factor | Training after | Note                           |
| ----- | ---------- | ---------------: | -----------: | ------------: | -------------: | ------------------------------ |
| 1     | Apprentice |              100 |          80% |          0.30 |       2 months | automatic                      |
| 2     | Apprentice |              100 |          90% |          0.40 |       2 months | automatic                      |
| 3     | Apprentice |               80 |         100% |          0.60 |       4 months | automatic                      |
| 4     | Journeyman |               50 |         105% |          0.90 |              – | training complete              |
| 5     | Journeyman |               50 |         120% |          1.00 |              – |                                |
| 6     | Journeyman |               50 |         130% |          1.10 |       6 months | Master training, €3,000        |
| 7     | Master     |              100 |         150% |          1.20 |              – | leadership role                |
| 8     | Master     |              110 |         160% |          1.40 |              – | leadership role                |
| 9     | Master     |              120 |         170% |          1.50 |       8 months | Director training, €6,000      |
| 10    | Director   |              max |            – |          2.20 |              – | top tier, reduces property tax |

If you put a Director into a regular shift slot, they work with the productivity of a level 9 Master (170%).

### Train or hire?

Employees you train yourself stay cheaper, because skilled workers from the job market ask for 10–70% more base salary:

| Tier                 | Trained yourself | From the job market |
| -------------------- | ---------------: | ------------------: |
| Journeyman (level 4) |      €72 / month |     €79–122 / month |
| Master (level 7)     |      €96 / month |    €106–163 / month |
| Director (level 10)  |     €176 / month |    €194–299 / month |

- **Availability:** Only about 6% of skilled-worker applicants are Masters or Directors. Highly qualified external specialists are therefore rather rare.
- **Time:** From apprentice to Journeyman takes about 3 years, to Director about 8 years, if they work every month.
- **Training fees:** €3,000 (Master) and €6,000 (Director).

So hire apprentices early, as soon as your first factory reaches level 3, and assign them regularly.

### Leadership roles

Besides the regular shift slots, a factory gets a **Master slot** from level 3 and an additional **Director slot** at level 5.

- Only a Master (level 7–9) can fill the Master slot. They work in the day shift and add their high productivity there.
- Only a Director (level 10) can fill the Director slot. They do not actively work in production but take care of administration and reduce their factory's property tax by **75%**.
- Without the required leaders, the factory does not produce (see [Factories & levels](#factories--levels)).
- Employees in the Master or Director slot do not take up a regular shift slot.
- When an employee goes into training, they leave their shift or leadership slot. After training you have to assign them again.

### Dismissal, retirement & training deadlines

Employees age and retire at **65**, without severance.

A dismissal costs a **severance of €50 per month employed**, paid at the end of the notice period. Depending on the level, a notice period applies during which the employee keeps working and receives their salary:

| Employee        | Notice period       |
| --------------- | ------------------- |
| Seasonal worker | end of month        |
| Apprentice      | cannot be dismissed |
| Journeyman      | 3 months            |
| Master          | 6 months            |
| Director        | 9 months            |

While the notice period is running, you can withdraw the dismissal for a fee:

| Employee        |     Fee |
| --------------- | ------: |
| Seasonal worker |  €1,500 |
| Journeyman      |  €3,000 |
| Master          |  €6,000 |
| Director        | €10,000 |

Apprentices cannot be dismissed, but they need attention. The regular training period is 3 years. If an apprentice has not finished training after **4 years**, a €500 penalty is charged. After **5 years** they give up and look for another farm, which costs you a €2,500 penalty plus €15,000 severance. So assign apprentices regularly so they gain enough experience.

### Sickness & resignation

Now and then employees call in sick for **1 to 4 days**. Some are more robust than others, and from age 50 they get sick a bit more often. A sick employee leaves their shift or leadership slot but keeps getting paid. In the Employees tab you find them in the "Sick" section, and the overview shows a warning. After they recover you have to assign them again yourself. If the Master is off sick, the factory stands still for that time unless you have a suitable replacement.

Employees can also resign on their own, mostly when they have had no shift and no leadership slot for a while. Employees with 5 or more years in the company stay more loyal; apprentices and employees in training never resign. The normal notice period applies and you pay no severance. A **counter-offer** may keep the employee: you offer **10 to 100%** more base salary. Whether they accept is not guaranteed, and you only get **one negotiation attempt**.

## Factories & levels

Factories develop too. A factory gains experience while at least one recipe is running and employees are working in it, up to **10 XP per month**. Once it has enough experience for the next level, you get a notification and can buy the upgrade in the FabriX menu's shift plan (key **X**).

After the purchase, the factory is renovated, rebuilt and enlarged. Production stands still during this time and cannot be switched on; afterwards you have to reactivate it in the production menu.

If you sell a factory, the employees working there become free and you can then assign them to other factories.

A higher level gives you:

- more storage capacity for input and output goods
- more employee slots per shift and therefore more potential productivity
- from level 3, room for apprentices and a Master

| Level | Experience needed | Storage factor | Upgrade cost | Renovation | Slots per shift | Required leaders  |
| ----- | ----------------: | -------------: | -----------: | ---------: | --------------: | ----------------- |
| 1     |                 – |           1.0x |            – |          – |               1 | –                 |
| 2     |                80 |           2.0x |      €30,000 |    1 month |               2 | –                 |
| 3     |               110 |           3.5x |      €80,000 |   2 months |               3 | Master            |
| 4     |               210 |           5.0x |     €120,000 |   3 months |               4 | Master            |
| 5     |               330 |           7.5x |     €250,000 |   3 months |               5 | Master + Director |

> **Important:** From level 3, a factory **only produces with a Master**; from level 5 it **also needs a Director**. Without them, productivity is 0%. Plan your leaders before upgrading.

## Costs & finance

FabriX replaces the base game's fixed production costs with real operating and staff costs:

| Cost type          | Billing                                                                      |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wages & salaries   | monthly, depending on category and level                                     |
| Water              | monthly by consumption, proportional to running recipes, €5.45 per m³        |
| Electricity        | monthly by consumption, proportional to running recipes, €0.35 per kWh       |
| Transport          | daily, costs of automatic goods distribution                                 |
| Property tax       | yearly, €80–800 per factory (set randomly on purchase), −75% with a Director |
| Recruitment        | €250 per hire, fee for withdrawn dismissals                                  |
| Training           | €3,000 (Master training), €6,000 (Director training)                         |
| Severance payments | at the end of a notice period or when an apprentice gives up                 |
| Penalty payments   | when an apprentice takes too long to finish training                         |
| Upgrades           | €30,000–250,000, booked as building purchase                                 |

Water and electricity are only charged while a production is running. Fixed electricity and water consumption values have been set for the base game factories. Factories from other mods use random amounts of electricity and water. These cost types appear as their own rows in the finance overview in the pause menu, so you can see exactly where your money goes. A factory's upgrade costs are booked like a base-game building purchase.

## Limitations

- In multiplayer, notifications appear in the host's or server's language.
- FabriX has been successfully tested on dedicated servers.
- FabriX was created in my spare time, so unfortunately bugs can occur. I do try to test FabriX thoroughly. However, this does not completely rule out bugs.

## Background

I have played Farming Simulator for many versions and seen how much mods shape the experience. For productions, though, FS25 still had a lot of untapped potential for me: the factories worked, but stayed passive and asked for hardly any decisions.

The FS22 mod "Production Revamp" showed that more is possible, but it still lacked the depth I wanted. I wanted productions to become part of my decisions: Who works in the factory? Which qualification is needed? When does an upgrade pay off? What does the operation cost each month?

When GIANTS announced the mod contest, I thought: now is the right time to try my hand at my first own mod.

That is how FabriX came about, built over many hours of spare time with a lot of learning about modding and game mechanics.
